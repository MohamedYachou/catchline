# Voyage legs — what the sail out and the sail home can be

Status: PROPOSAL from Fable for Mo, 2026-10-24. Not design authority until Mo approves. Part A is a
catalogue of everything the two legs could be, well beyond preparation, with fit and cost against
the current design. Part B builds one direction out in full (plan, execute, fish, finish, two
trips a day). Part C is the recommended combination. When approved, this amends BLUEPRINT,
MASTER-PLAN, MAP-PLAN, CARDS-AND-SPEEDS, UI-SYSTEM and ART-PLAN as listed in B11. Every number is
provisional.

The question: travel between harbour and ground is 10–20 s because waiting is boring. Mo wants
the legs to be real parts of the loop and is open to a different kind of management on them.

The test used throughout: **does it give a role something to do on the leg that changes the
fishing, the sale or the next trip?** Flavour alone does not earn a longer leg.

---

## Part A — The catalogue

Material is what the leg works on. Leg is Out, Home or Both. Fit names the current rule it rests
on or brushes against. Cost is code + art, rough. Verdict is one line.

### A1. Fish while moving — the legs as a different fishery

**1. Trolling.** A gear family that only works under way: lines towed astern catch pelagics. The
species exist already (mackerel white, flying fish white, barracuda blue, yellowfin tuna purple,
swordfish gold; add bonito green). A fisher with speciality Troll (new card, tier 3) runs one or two
stern lines end to end: lure on, pay out, strike on the bite, haul, into the troll basket. Bites
depend on boat speed: there is a trolling speed below cruise, so trolling makes the leg longer;
the throttle becomes a fishing dial. Lures from Supplies or tied aboard; lure colour decides the
class ceiling the way bait does. Events: shark takes the troll (lose the lure), tangle when
turning hard (the route's corners matter). The sorter has work outbound for the first time, so
the first wall of the day is sorting, not hauling. Material: fish. Leg: Both. Fit: fisher does one
gear family end to end; bait decides what bites; "the boat never moves while fishing" applies to
anchored gear and stays true. Does not brush the pots rejection (that was traps on a string).
Cost: one gear family, stern station on skiff and up, species-by-route pools, one card. Verdict:
**the strongest single idea for making a leg worth its length; fish are the game's material.**

**2. Pots along the route.** Drop a string of cages at marked points on the way out (the boat
stops 5 s per pot), lift them on the way home. Soak time becomes the whole fishing phase, so the
yield is larger than the 65 s cage cycle (crab, lobster). The home leg becomes a haul burst that
hits the sort table exactly when the table has time. Material: fish, homeward. Leg: Both (drop,
lift). Fit: **brushes MASTER-PLAN A1.4 "pots on a string worked by a moving boat", rejected for
ever.** This version stops at every pot and lifts them by hand; Mo must rule whether that is the
same thing. Tier 5 (cages exist). Cost: waypoint stops in BoatSailor, pot markers on the chart,
a pot string prop. Verdict: elegant if the rejection does not cover it; drop it if it does.

**3. Berley the approach.** Throw chum astern on the last 20 s before anchoring; the ground's bite
wait runs ×0.6 for the first 90 s, a self-made School passes. Costs 5 chum and a deckhand's time.
Material: chum. Leg: Out. Fit: chum as a ground buff instead of a bait, same product. Tier 4.
Cost: small. Verdict: cheap and very fishing; take it.

**4. Jig stops at night.** A 30 s stop at a lit buoy mid-route to jig squid for bait. Leg: Out.
Fit: it is a multi-stop trip (A2.7 rejects multi-stop). Verdict: no; trolling covers "fish on the
way" without a stop.

### A2. The sea as information — the legs as scouting

**5. Pass-by reading.** The navigator reads the richness bands of every ground zone the route
passes within 40 m. Route choice becomes a choice of what you learn: the long way round the island
scouts two grounds, the short way one. The chart shows bands with an age ("seen this morning",
"yesterday"). Material: information. Leg: Both. Fit: extends the M4 navigator readout; not a fish
finder (A1.4), it is a person reading water. Cost: small, data already exists. Verdict: take it;
it makes the island layout matter twice.

**6. Fog of war on a new map.** On the first day at a map, grounds are unknown until sailed near;
night pools are only revealed at night. The first trips to each of the five maps become a discovery
loop. Material: information. Leg: Out. Cost: chart state per map. Verdict: take it for maps 2–5;
the tutorial map starts known.

**7. Surface signs.** While sailing, birds working, bait balls, slicks and colour changes appear
as world objects; tapping one marks a temporary hot spot (one voyage, bonus pool). A gold navigator
spots more and earlier. Material: information turned into a ground. Leg: Both. Fit: "Birds working"
exists as an event; this makes it a place on the water. Cost: a few world sprites, a temp ground
record. Verdict: take it; it makes the player look at the sea.

**8. The weather bet.** Weather builds visibly on the way out; the navigator calls odds ("squall
by noon, two in three"); the captain presses on or turns back with half the wages refunded. Puts
the squall decision on the leg instead of a random roll at the ground. Material: risk. Leg: Out.
Cost: sky state, one modal. Verdict: take it; it is the only honest way to make weather a choice.

### A3. Logistics and side economy — the boat is a boat

**9. Errands.** The harbour board posts small jobs: run oil to the lighthouse, carry mail and
supplies to the island cottage, ferry a fisheries scientist to the reef (she pays with a full
richness read), tow a loose buoy back. Detours cost time against return-by; pay is coins, rep, a
chest or information. Material: cargo, time. Leg: Both. Fit: uses the islands MAP-PLAN places for
play and the lighthouse model that exists. Cost: a board, 6–8 errand types, a cargo crate prop.
Verdict: good mid-game texture, after the fishing core; it is what makes the map a place.

**10. Tender at sea.** A fish carrier anchored at the harbour mouth buys at −15%: land empty,
skip the quay, sail again sooner; or carry home for full price. The Collector's yacht at the far
island wants one special product today at ×2. Material: fish, time. Leg: Home. Fit: buyers (M5);
"no price drop for volume" holds, it is a different buyer. Cost: one boat prop, a sale variant.
Verdict: take the carrier once two sailings a day exist; it is the time-versus-coins lever.

**11. Flotsam and salvage.** Drifting lost gear (a rig, a net float), crates (bait, oil, a chest),
a drifting pot string that belongs to someone (taking it is remembered by the inspector). The
navigator spots, a deckhand hooks it with a boathook (8 s), the boat slows briefly. Material:
loot. Leg: Both. Cost: small. Verdict: take it lightly, one roll per leg at most.

**12. Towing.** A broken-down boat on the way home: tow for a fee at ×0.7 speed and more fuel, or
leave it and lose rep. Leg: Home. Verdict: one event in the Return stage, not a system.

### A4. A living map — other boats

**13. Rival fleet.** Two or three AI boats per map with daily habits. They sail to grounds and
drain them; the first boat to anchor gets the fresh richness; they anchor near you when you are on
a rich spot, which makes the existing "Rival anchors nearby" event emergent instead of rolled. You
see them under way, so the passage is a race (throttle, fuel). Radio chatter, trades of
information, a rivalry contract ("out-fish Vikram's boat today"). Material: competition. Leg:
Both. Fit: richness drain and recovery (M4) already model the effect; this gives it a face. Cost:
boat AI on SeaNav routes, 3 skipper cards, chatter lines. Verdict: **the single best "the map is
alive" idea; schedule it after M6 as a content layer, design the ground data for it now.**

**14. Inspector at sea.** The inspector boards on the way home, heave-to 30 s. Over-quota fish
can be dumped before boarding: fine avoided, catch wasted, rep lost if seen. Leg: Home. Fit: the
inspector exists (M5/M6). Verdict: move the inspector here; the dumping choice is optional and
should be discussed, it is dark.

**15. Wildlife on the way.** Dolphins ride the bow (×1.1 speed), seals follow you home and raid
your lines tomorrow unless shooed, a whale stops the boat for 20 s and gives a card a line.
Material: flavour with small effects. Leg: Both. Cost: models exist for most. Verdict: take two or
three; the rest is dressing.

### A5. The crew between jobs — people, not stations

**16. Mentoring.** A higher-level card teaches a lower one on the sail: XP to the pupil per leg;
the teacher does nothing else that leg. Progression beyond kilograms landed, and the reason a
maxed late card sails on a small boat (A1.5 records that case without a why). Material: time.
Leg: Both. Cost: small. Verdict: take it; it gives gold cards a job when their gear is not aboard.

**17. Rapport.** Pairs that sail together build rapport; purple teamwork abilities run at 50% for
strangers and 100% at full rapport; "Won't work adjacent" only happens to zero-rapport pairs; the
captain can sit with a pair on the leg to mediate (a job). The placement puzzle gets a history and
the roster rotation gets a cost. Material: relationships. Leg: Both. Fit: BLUEPRINT §14 teamwork
abilities. Cost: a pair table in the save, a few lines. Verdict: take it after cards (M2) land.

**18. Injuries and treatment.** Hook in hand and crab pinch heal on the way home with a first-aid
kit from Supplies, or the card sits out tomorrow. Leg: Home. Verdict: small, include with events.

**19. Card moments.** One line per card per leg, tap to dismiss; sometimes a choice ("Dirk wants to
try the drop-off": agree and his strike runs +5 this trip). Material: attachment. Cost: text and
portraits that exist. Verdict: take it; it is how a Clash Royale roster becomes a RimWorld crew.

**20. Shanty.** The crew sing on the way home when the hold is over 60%. Pure feel. Verdict: yes
as sound, never as a buff (free buffs are the rejected income-multiplier class).

### A6. The captain's desk — management on the leg

**21. Debrief on the way home.** One slab: the trip's story (which stage backed up and when, fish
lost and why, who carried most, coins in and out) and one recommendation for the next plan. The
learning loop of a management game lives here, not in the firefight; nobody reads numbers during a
net burst. Feeds "Same as last trip" with a suggested change. Material: the trip's data. Leg: Home.
Fit: UI-SYSTEM §4 bans charts and per-minute costs; this is words and fish icons. Cost: the
StageReport history already exists in the read-models. Verdict: take it; it is the cheapest big
one.

**22. Meta on the leg.** Chests open and wildcards are spent on the way home; the sail is the
"between matches" where Clash Royale opens chests. Placement, not a mechanic. Leg: Home. Verdict:
take it; it turns the return into the reward beat.

**23. Radio deals.** Call ahead to buyers (price windows), accept rush orders on the way out,
cancel a contract for a fee from sea. Leg: Both. Fit: Mira's gold Radio and M5 contracts. Verdict:
take it; detailed in B6.

**24. Tide table.** Tides change the routes: the sandbar's gully opens at high water, the harbour
bar slows the approach at low water, the kelp shallows widen. Departure timing matters and the two
sailings of a day sail different seas. Material: time. Leg: Both. Fit: MAP-PLAN shallows and
routing; a per-map tide curve over the day clock. Cost: a time-varying shallow ring in SeaNav.
Verdict: take it for Sandbar and Harbour mouth; it is a second rhythm in the day.

### A7. Structural

**25. Asynchronous legs.** On mobile a leg can run while the app is closed and notify on arrival.
Verdict: later; it changes retention design, not the loop.

**26. Two-boat operations.** At tier 5 the skiff runs as a tender between the big boat and the
quay. Verdict: tier 6 material.

**27. The passage as a world.** The five maps join into one coast with long passages between
harbours; a new harbour is World 2. Verdict: prestige material (A1.5), not v1.

| Needs 60–120 s to work | Works in 30 s |

## Part B — One direction built out: plan, execute, fish, finish; two trips a day

This is Mo's preparation idea designed in full, so it can be judged against the catalogue. B1 says
why it is the same game; B5.1 shows the "choose your wall" dynamic with real numbers.

## B0. The idea in one paragraph

A voyage has four parts. **Plan** in harbour with no clock running: pick the ground, choose what
rides in each deck slot, place the crew, decide who eats, what bait and chum go aboard, and (tier 5)
how power is spent. **Sail out**: the crew executes that plan against a time budget equal to the
travel time; unfinished preparation spills into fishing time. **Fish**: the existing labour chain,
now shaped by the plan — fed crew are faster, rigged gear is ready, lit stations are lit, tuned
machines run hot — so the player has chosen which stage will be the wall this trip. **Sail home**:
the table and sort table finish the backlog, the throttle trades fuel for time, the radio sells
ahead, and leftover whites become tomorrow's chum. A game day has two sailings, so the second plan
reacts to the first catch, the roster's B-team matters, and the morning trip can produce the chum
the night trip needs.

Design principle: **the deck never stops; only the material changes.** Outbound the material is
provisions, bait, gear and information. At the ground it is fish. Homeward it is the backlog and
the sale. The job system (MASTER-PLAN B3) runs all three with new job kinds, no second simulation.

---

## B1. Why this is the same game, not a second one

The thesis (A1.3) is: delegate, the bottleneck moves, repeat. In the fishing phase the bottleneck
moves when you hire and assign. The plan phase adds a second dial that moves it **before** the trip:
CARDS-AND-SPEEDS §7 deliberately balances the stages within 20% of each other at tiers 3–5, so any
+25% applied to one stage pushes the wall to the next. Rations, rigging and engineer tuning are
exactly such pushes, paid for in coins and outbound crew-time. The player chooses the wall, then
survives it. That is a strictly richer version of the current design, and it needs the stages to
stay balanced the way CARDS-AND-SPEEDS §7 already specifies.

Reference points (checked 2026-10-24): Dave the Diver splits the day into slots where the dive
feeds the restaurant; Darkest Dungeon's camp is a provisions-and-buff choice taken before the
fight, and players rate generic camp buffs below class skills (so rations here stay weaker than
card abilities); Frostpunk and Oregon Trail both set rations against pace; real halibut crews cut
bait and bait every hook before leaving port for a 24-hour opening, and small longliners bait during
the steam out, because bait loses over half its value within five hours. Catchline's version: bait
on the way out, not the day before.

## B2. The day: two sailings

| Window | Tier | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Morning sailing | 0+ | the tutorial runs one trip and ends the day |
| Second sailing | 0+ day; from T2 may be a night trip | night needs the lantern (BLUEPRINT §11) |

- The sim clock runs during sailing and fishing, not during planning or sale. Each sailing has a
  **return-by** time; miss it and the day ends with one trip. That gives "head home" a reason
  other than a full hold, which the current design lacks (BLUEPRINT §1 lists "decide when to go
  home" as the captain's job but nothing pushes it).
- Wages per trip are **half** today's tierBase (T1 4 · T2 8 · T3 15 · T4 30 · T5 60), so a crew
  that sails both trips costs what one trip costs today; a rotated card costs half. "This trip
  costs N" on the plan screen stays one number and now includes provisions.
- Hold caps are unchanged. Nobody has to fill the hold; the tier-0–2 value loss over time
  (A3.16) already favours a short trip, and from T3 the reasons for two trips are the second plan,
  night species, "by tonight" contracts and the chum pipeline (B6).
- Roster is 2× berths (A4.21). With two sailings the B-team gets its reason: B7 fatigue.
- XP per trip uses the existing kg formula; two half trips land the same kg as one full one.

Timing target per trip: plan 30–60 s of taps · sail out 30–90 s · fish 4–6 min · sail home
30–90 s · sale 20 s → 6–9 min. A day is 13–18 min, which is the A1.2 session.

## B3. Phase 1 — Plan (harbour, no clock)

One screen replaces the Departure sheet of UI-SYSTEM §3: the **Deck plan**. Top-down deck plate
drawn from the hull's station file (positions already exist), with:

1. **Route card.** Destination ground (from the chart), day/night, ETA, and a **prep budget bar**:
   "Sail 60 s · planned work 75 s" turns `Warn` when the plan does not fit. Near ground 30 s, mid
   60 s, far 90 s (per map; hull speed tuned so these hold on every hull). Far grounds are richer
   (M4 richness tables already differ per ground), so distance is a trade: more preparation time,
   less fishing time before return-by.
2. **Slots.** Each hull has a few **convertible slots**; everything else is fixed as today:
   rail slot = rod by day or jig by night (T2+); module slot = net, or cage set / line set at T5.
   The player picks what rides in each slot; a fisher rigs it outbound (B4). Not a free deck
   builder: slot count and allowed kinds come from the station file.
3. **Crew placement.** Fishers to a station; deckhands' primary/secondary duty (exists) and, at
   T5, a home table. Placement matters because purple abilities are teamwork with neighbours
   (BLUEPRINT §14: Lise lights the neighbouring station, Noor pre-stacks for the hauler on her
   route, Kofi tends the neighbouring net). Who sits next to whom is the puzzle.
4. **Rations.** A plate slot per card and the captain (B7). Supplies sells the items.
5. **Bait and chum.** Shop bait (exists) plus the chum already in the bin and any whites kept from
   the last sale for grinding (B6).
6. **Power plan (T5).** Which machines are on, and which one the engineer tunes (B8).
7. **Forecast.** The navigator's line for this trip: ground richness bands (M4 report) plus one or
   two likely events, accuracy by level (B4.3). Counter-measures are plan items that cost outbound
   time: lid the baskets (gull raid), lash the deck (squall), spare rig to hand (snag).
8. **Same as last trip.** One button restores the previous plan for this ground. A plan that
   changes nothing is a 10-second screen; a re-plan is a minute. This is the answer to "two plans a
   day is admin".

Primary button: **Cast off**. Phone layout: the deck plate shrinks to a strip and the items become
a list; same components (UI-SYSTEM §2).

## B4. Phase 2 — Sail out (sim clock = travel time)

The boat follows its route (MAP-PLAN §4, built). The deck graph ticks with **prep jobs**; the
scheduler gives each worker the plan items that belong to them, highest value first. Every crew
member has `travel seconds` of time; the budget bar on the plan screen is the sum.

### B4.1 Prep jobs (base seconds at skill 100, level 1; speed model of CARDS-AND-SPEEDS §1)

| Job | Who (skill used) | Base | Needs | Output | If unfinished at arrival |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand out packed lunch | anyone | 0 | lunch bought | card is Fed (B7) | — |
| Brew coffee | anyone (Table) | 5 per cup | kettle (T2), coffee bought | cups in the galley, used by tap during fishing | brewed when someone is idle |
| Cook hot meal | deckhand or captain (Table) | 15 per portion | galley (T3 hull), ingredients | card is Fed | cooked later; the buff arrives late |
| Rig rod | fisher (Rods) | 8 | rail slot | rod ready | fisher rigs at the ground first |
| Rig jig and lamp | fisher (Jigs) | 15 | rail slot, night, oil | lit jig station | unlit until rigged |
| Rig net | fisher (Nets) | 30 | module slot | net ready to set | first set delayed 30 s |
| Pre-bait line set | fisher (Lines) | 20 | chum in the bin | line drops on arrival at full yield | first drop at half yield |
| Pre-bait cage set | fisher (Cages) | 12 | bait | cage ready | first drop delayed |
| Grind chum | deckhand (Table) | 3.0 per unit | kept whites/greens/blues, table (T4) | chum in the bin | ground at the table later, competing with orders |
| Light lantern / deck lights | anyone | 5 per station | night, oil | station lit | unlit (×0.7 sort/haul, BLUEPRINT §11) |
| Lid the baskets | deckhand (Haul) | 4 per basket | basket lids (Supplies upgrade) | gull raid takes nothing | lids go on when idle |
| Lash the deck | deckhand (Haul) | 10 | — | squall takes no loose fish this trip | not lashed |
| Tune a machine (T5) | engineer | 40 | power on | B8 | untuned |

Rules: a fisher only rigs their own gear family at speciality speed (other families at 50/65/80,
same as fishing). Meals are the only prep that is never "lost": a late meal is a late buff. Nothing
here is a chore repeated every cycle; it is set-up, once per trip, and skipping any of it is legal.
"Bait never blocks fishing" (A5.26) holds: scraps stay free and infinite; pre-baiting is about the
**first** drop, not whether lines fish.

### B4.2 What the player does outbound

Watches the plan execute (crew visibly cooking, rigging, lighting), answers one or two events,
takes radio calls, and may re-plot to another ground on the navigator's word. Ops and Crew screens
are open under way. No new input kind: the five verbs of BLUEPRINT §13.

### B4.3 Outbound events (new stage "Passage", MASTER-PLAN M6 schema)

| Event | D/N, T | Effect → answer |
|---|---|---|
| Fog in the channel | D, T1 | route slows: +20 s travel, more prep time, less fishing before return-by → accept, or re-plot nearer |
| Following sea | D/N, T1 | −15 s travel: less prep time → re-plan on the fly or arrive unready |
| Birds working ahead | D, T2 | navigator offers a different ground now → re-plot (free) |
| Left the worms on the quay | D, T2 | shop bait gone; scraps only → turn back (40 s) or accept |
| Engine knock | D/N, T2 | half speed → captain 15 s fix (T2–4) or engineer (T5), or accept |
| Galley fire | D/N, T3 | one meal lost, cook out 20 s → reassign cook |
| Stowaway gulls | D, T2 | unlidded baskets lose bait → shoo (tap) |
| Radio: price spike / rush order / weather warning | D/N, T1–2 | existing M6 events, now rolled here → change orders / keep rule / plan |

Forecast accuracy (navigator): level 1 names one likely event correctly 50% of the time; level 30
names two at 90%. Gold Radio (Mira) adds the market calls. This is the planning phase's
information input; without it the plan is a guess, with it the plan is a puzzle.

## B5. Phase 3 — Fish (the chain, shaped by the plan)

Unchanged mechanics (BLUEPRINT §2–§8, §12–§13). What the plan changes:

- Fed cards run at +15% (lunch) or +25% (hot meal) on every job this trip. Coffee is a tap-to-use
  consumable: +40% for 60 s on one card (an event answer of the "spend a consumable" kind).
- Rigged gear starts fishing on arrival; unrigged gear costs its rig time first.
- Lit stations are lit; lids make gull raids harmless; a lashed deck makes squalls harmless to
  loose fish.
- A tuned machine runs hot all trip (B8).
- Relocation inside the map works as M4 (30 s move, gear in, one haul cycle lost); it is the only
  travel inside the fishing phase and stays short.

### B5.1 Choosing the wall — worked example, tier 3

CARDS-AND-SPEEDS §7 at tier 3: intake 26 fish/min peak (2 rods + a net), one sorter 33, one hauler
25. Hot meals cost 5 coins and 15 s of a cook's outbound time each.

| Who eats | Intake | Sort | Haul | Wall this trip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nobody | 26 | 33 | 25 | hauling, mildly (today's intended wall) |
| sorter | 26 | 41 | 25 | hauling, hard: SortOut fills, hauler never idle |
| hauler | 26 | 33 | 31 | nothing backs up; hauler idles 15% |
| both fishers and net fisher | 32 | 33 | 25 | hauling, badly: pile on deck in the net burst |
| hauler + fishers (4 meals) | 32 | 33 | 31 | all three within 5%: Busy everywhere, no wall, 20 coins and 60 s of the sorter's outbound time |

The hidden cost is the cook: at tier 3 she is the sorter, so four meals mean the net fisher rigs
alone and the first set is late. At tier 4 the table hand cooks, and every portion is 15 s of chum
or oil not ground. Rations never beat the CARDS-AND-SPEEDS §7 balance for free; they move it.

## B6. Phase 4 — Sail home (sim clock = travel time)

The one leg with fish aboard. Rules:

- **Processing continues.** The `Sailing` flag in the scheduler blocks table work today; on the home
  leg the table and sort table run. The sail is table-minutes, the T4 scarce resource; "head home
  with a backlog" becomes a real option.
- **Throttle (motor hulls, T2+).** Dead slow ×0.6 speed and ×0.5 fuel per minute · Cruise ×1 ·
  Full ahead ×1.5 speed and ×2.5 fuel. Fuel is a 10 or 20 minute budget (Supplies), so running
  hard for the dinner window can leave the evening trip short of fuel. Rowboat has no throttle.
- **Call ahead (radio, T3+; gold Radio earlier).** The Restaurant's dinner window 17:00–20:00 pays
  ×1.5 instead of ×1.3 for blue+ named species; the Cannery closes at 18:00. Optional (decision 6).
- **Chum carry-over.** A table order `Chum ∞` at the bottom of the list grinds unsold whites on the
  way home; chum persists in the bin overnight (cap 20). The sale screen gets "Keep n whites" for
  grinding on the next outbound. Trade: 3 coins at the Bait shop per white, or 1 chum. This is how
  a morning rod trip feeds a night line trip at Deep channel.
- **Fatigue decision.** The home-leg crew rail shows who will be Tired for the next sailing (B7),
  so the next plan's rotation is decided with the catch in view.
- **Return-by.** The HUD shows the time against the next sailing's return-by; arriving late ends
  the day.

Return events: squall (loose fish lost unless lashed), cargo shift (re-stow, exists), buyer calls
(window offer), rival racing you to the quay (first in takes the window), harbour fog (slow
approach, +20 s).

## B7. Rations and fatigue

| Ration | Tier | Cost | Prep | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Packed lunch | 0 | 3 coins | none | Fed: +15% every job this trip |
| Coffee | 2 (kettle, Supplies 40 coins) | 2 coins per cup | brew 5 s | +40% for 60 s on one card, by tap |
| Hot meal | 3 (galley prop on the compact hull and up) | 5 coins ingredients | cook 15 s per portion | Fed: +25% every job this trip |

- Captain eats too. Tier 0's first contact with the system is "buy a sandwich, sort faster"; tier
  1's first allocation decision is "you or Noor".
- **Fatigue:** a card that sails the second trip of a day without having eaten on the first is
  **Tired** on the second: −20% every job. Tired clears after a trip ashore. Roster shows the word.
- Recommendation: **buff in the voyage, fatigue across the day**, never "unfed = slower" inside a
  single trip. An unfed penalty every trip reads as a tax, which is the complaint about waiting
  that started this. Alternative for Mo: fed = baseline, unfed = −15% (cheaper to balance, harsher).
- Rations are weaker than card abilities on purpose (Darkest Dungeon lesson): a gold signature
  changes what a job is; a meal changes how fast.
- No food beyond this. No hunger meter, no morale, no quality grades. Fish are never eaten.

## B8. Engineer tuning (tier 5; room for tier 6)

The engineer's outbound job is to tune **one** powered system for the trip (gold Olaf: two). 40 s.

| Tune | Effect this trip | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Winch | net set 2.0 → 1.5 s, net haul 5.0 → 3.5 s | a net trip |
| Line hauler | line raise 2 → 1 s, LineOut basket +2 | a line trip |

## B13. Acceptance (bot, per tier, 30 days of two trips)

1. With nobody fed, the most-backed-up stage is the B7 wall (today's test still passes).
2. Feeding one stage moves the wall to the stage B5.1 predicts, at tiers 3–5.
3. A bot that plans for the ground (right rations, gear rigged for it, counters for the forecast)
   earns at least 25% more per day than a bot that sails "same as last trip" blind.
4. Outbound idle time per crew member ≤ 30% at a mid-distance ground when the plan uses the
   budget; a plan over budget finishes its remainder at the ground and delays fishing by exactly
   the overrun.
5. Home-leg processing: a tier-4 bot that heads home with ten fish in TableIn arrives with ≤ 2.
6. A card that sails twice unfed is Tired on the second trip; fed or rested, it is not.
7. Chum ground on the home leg is in the bin on the next outbound; "Keep n whites" removes them
   from the sale and they are ground outbound.
8. Missing return-by ends the day with one trip and no crash.

## Part C — Recommended combination for v1

The rule that picks: **every role gets a leg job, and both legs end in something the next phase
uses.**

| Role | Sail out | Sail home |
|---|---|---|
| Captain | plan executes (B3); weather bet (A8) | debrief (A21); chests and wildcards (A22); throttle (B6) |
| Navigator | pass-by reading (A5), surface signs (A7), fog of war on new maps (A6) | logs what she saw for tomorrow's chart; call-ahead (A23) |
| Fisher | trolling (A1) or rigging (B4) — not both, that is the choice | trolling home, or lifting pots (A2) if Mo allows them |
| Deckhand | cook, grind chum, lids, lash (B4); sort the troll catch | finish the table, sort the pots or troll catch, Chum ∞ (B6) |
| Engineer (T5) | tune one machine (B8) | nothing new; he hauls at 50 as today |
| Everyone | card moments (A19), mentoring (A16) when idle | rapport grows (A17) |

Over days: two sailings (B2), rations and fatigue (B7), the rival fleet (A13) as the M6+ content
layer, errands (A9) and the tender (A10) once two sailings exist, tides (A24) on two maps.

Leg length with this set: 60 s mid-distance, 30–90 s by ground. The leg earns it because the
fisher is fishing (trolling), the navigator is reading, the deck is working the plan or the
catch, and the captain is deciding (weather, throttle, debrief). Nothing on the list is a wait.

Not taken: jig stops (multi-stop), shanty buffs, berth queues, dumping fish (unless Mo wants the
dark choice), asynchronous legs, two-boat operations.

Build order: B (plan, two sailings, rations, home-leg processing, Deck plan screen) → A1 trolling
+ A5/A7 reading → A21 debrief + A22 meta + A19 moments → A8 weather bet + A3 berley + A11 flotsam
→ A13 rival fleet → A9 errands + A10 tender → A24 tides → A16/A17 mentoring and rapport.
