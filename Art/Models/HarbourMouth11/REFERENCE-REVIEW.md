## What the original actually shows

- **Water:** saturated clear turquoise with substantial depth and colour range.
  Broad irregular light patches overlap smaller refracted-looking facets;
  darker submerged forms and fish remain readable through the surface. Bright
  broken contact foam follows individual pilings and hull intersections.
  Long curved streaks have direction and variation rather than uniform rings.
  Preserve quiet areas without reducing the sea to flat teal or dense noise.
- **Working harbour:** close, warm timber with readable plank joints, bevelled
  edges, end caps, fasteners and structural piling shadows. Rope wraps and
  hanging loops join the structure physically. Barrels, crates, nets, fishing
  gear and a sign form purposeful groups. Richness comes from overlapping
  materials, believable construction and contact shadows at gameplay scale.
- **Boat and people:** honey deck, ivory rails, deep blue hull and restrained
  orange highlights, with substantial bevels and soft grounded shadows.
  Gear reads as useful objects with distinct materials and thickness. Preserve
  existing rowboat gameplay/deck/socket contracts; this reference's larger
  vessel is a visual-language guide, not permission to replace the starter hull.
- **UI:** warm cream rounded panels, crisp deep-navy typography/icons and
  strongly illustrated dimensional coin/crate motifs. Few well-spaced groups
  leave the boat dominant. Bold hierarchy and generous spacing matter as much
  as matching colour. Generic cream rectangles alone do not meet the target.

## Consequences for the next coastal art pass

Prioritize the camera-visible landing/jetty as a convincing working-harbour
focal point: timber construction, end grain/edges, fitted posts, rope attachment,
small grouped working props and contact-shadow opportunities. Terrain should
support that place, with attached strata, varied silhouettes and grounded
vegetation; adding disconnected rocks does not solve the composition.

Judge detail at the exact desktop and landscape-phone gameplay cameras, not
only in close renders. Use the original reference alongside the new render;
use the previous runtime/ART-011 only to identify remaining gaps. Keep clear
boat/cast/interaction areas and the authored seabed's depth variation.

Windows owns actual water, shaders, lighting, UI and runtime evaluation. Its
current test is the actual adapted Boat Attack package from Plunder in scratch
with the orthographic camera. No package integration, visual acceptance or
runtime parity is implied by this art note. Preserve Plunder and its licenses.
